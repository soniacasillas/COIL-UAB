Evaluation report · October 2026

PhyloGenome COIL UAB × UNAM

Results from the GID’s first COIL pilot: participation, student experience and lessons for future editions.

5–30 October 2026Biodiversity and genomicsUAB × UNAM

Main takeaway. The pilot created a highly valued learning experience for the students who reached the end, particularly for its intercultural value and its contribution to understanding biodiversity and conservation genomics. At the same time, declining participation and fragile international teams show that future COILs need a stronger welcome process, sustained groups and clearer communication.

1The pilot at a glance

PhyloGenome COIL brought together students from Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona and the University of Namibia for four weeks of online activities, collaborative work, and an applied approach to biodiversity and conservation genomics.

UAB motivations

Students were especially interested in biodiversity and conservation in another context, scientific and intercultural exchange, conservation genomics, communication, and multidisciplinary teamwork.

UNAM motivations

Students emphasised conservation genomics, local species and knowledge, career and digital skills, international teamwork, and sharing their own context.

Availability declared

UAB mentioned classes, labs, placements and employment; UNAM mentioned final-year research, examinations and connectivity difficulties. Both groups nevertheless confirmed participation.

Initial interest versus sustained participation

The gap between universal initial confirmation and subsequent platform records suggests that registration should be complemented by an activation step before teams are finalised: platform access, a brief introduction and a first meaningful interaction with the group.

Measurable commitment, insufficient interaction

The average of 4.4 hours per week is close to the planned five hours. Yet only 1.85 hours, on average, were spent interacting with the team. Future design should reserve time, space and shared objectives for collaboration to actually take place.

6Qualitative reading of open responses

This synthesis identifies recurring themes and differences in emphasis; it does not reproduce individual comments. Responses confirm that value comes from combining scientific content, local contexts and intercultural contact, while requested improvements focus on communication and team composition.

What UAB students valued most

Learning about Namibian biodiversity and conservation, comparing cultural and scientific perspectives, and understanding the contribution of genomics to ecosystems. Moodle’s flexibility and remote knowledge-sharing were also valued.

What UNAM students valued most

Working with international students, the global dimension of biodiversity challenges, applied conservation genomics, and practising the synthesis of scientific information into accessible materials.

Improvements requested by UAB

Better group formation, a more direct communication channel, visual guides for tools and the game, more synchronous time for discussion and teamwork, and a way to ensure that nobody is left without an active team.

Improvements requested by UNAM

More stable technology or alternatives, more visible roles and deadlines, avoiding late group changes, more live work sessions and, in the longer term, opportunities for exchange or in-person meetings.

A shared message

Interest in repeating a COIL is very high at both universities. The programme has value and potential, but needs more structure to ensure that the international dimension materialises within every team.

7Coordination and facilitation feedback

Feedback from the coordinating teams and the facilitator reinforces student feedback and adds specific suggestions for design and follow-up.

Community before the start

Create a WhatsApp community before the project starts, so students can locate their group, solve quick questions and start interacting before the first session.

Motivation and recognition

Introduce individual and team points or badges throughout the course, recognising continuity, support for the group and shared goals.

Visible coordination

Hold weekly coordination meetings in Gather.town and strengthen communication between facilitators during video calls, allowing incidents to be addressed more quickly.

Groups: activate and stabilise

Do not build groups under pressure during the first session. Validate active participation first, anticipate drop-outs and avoid changes midway or late in the process; consider changing group configurations by activity when appropriate.

Platform and materials

Gather.town worked better than Teams for interaction and whiteboards were useful for sharing materials. Coordinators should practise using them and students should receive explicit onboarding on day one.

Synchronous sessions with real work

Consider two-hour sessions: one guided hour and one hour of free team work on the same platform. This may help establish the habit of talking, calling and progressing jointly during the week.

8Priorities for the next COIL

Combining the data, opinions and team feedback results in six priority actions, applicable to the next PhyloGenome COIL and transferable to other UAB COILs.

1. Mandatory pre-activation

Before assigning stable teams: access to the digital space, a minimum introduction, a technical check and active confirmation of availability.

2. Teams with a contingency plan

Create groups with critical mass, anticipate substitutes or alternative groupings, and agree when and how reassignment is triggered.

3. Guided first contact

Reserve time for students to meet, practise Gather.town, agree channels, roles, schedules and participation expectations.

4. Protected collaboration time

Include teamwork within synchronous sessions instead of relying only on asynchronous coordination between students.

5. One accessible communication system

Combine an informal community for alerts with an official space for materials, deadlines and evidence of work; provide an alternative if the main platform fails.

6. Weekly follow-up

Monitor activation, team contact and short milestones. Intervene quickly when silence, absence or unequal contribution appears.

9Methodology and limitations

Sources. Registration form (n = 30), Moodle activity records associated with applications, final survey (n = 10), and qualitative feedback from coordination and facilitation.

UAB–UNAM comparison. Differences are presented descriptively. Final responses come from small groups (n = 5 per university) and should not be read as generalisable statistical estimates.

Participation. Moodle records are indicators of platform activity, not a complete record of attendance, work carried out outside the platform, or causes of withdrawal.

Privacy. The report presents only aggregated data and thematic summaries. It includes no names, contact details or quotes attributable to individual people.
